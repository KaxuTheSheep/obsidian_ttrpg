Rifts are not conjured—they exploit natural weak points where dimensional proximity thins the spatial barrier. They can be forced open, stabilised, or theoretically closed.

## Weak Points & Formation
Weak points are cosmological accidents—determined by dimensional geometry, not engineering. High ambient mana density destabilises local spatial properties, thinning the barrier until it tears.

## Time Equilibrium Pressure
Connected worlds exert mutual time-rate pull. Mana acts as a passive buffer; without it, equilibrium propagates at light speed. With it, the effect slows to human running pace—a critical constraint that determined Solasfall's survival window.

## The Fallows Rift
Connects to a fast-time world (1 second outside ≈ 1 century experienced). The tear is permanent and self-sustaining. Propagation from the rift consumed half the planet at running speed before Solasfall received word. Mages could theoretically close it—but proximity is impossible because the Fallows itself is impassable. The distance that bought time is the same distance that makes intervention impossible.

## The Hollowdeep Rift
A natural weak point beneath Solasfall, connecting to the First Nexus (average time rate ≈ 7:1). The Riftconjurors do not create this—they force it open temporarily by channelling coordinated mana to overcome spatial resistance. This requires partially weakening the Veil because both operations compete for the same mana environment. The rift is held open for one hour every four weeks; during that hour, time flows equally between worlds. The four-week cycle is a practical capacity limit, not a chosen design.

## Existential Dependency
Mordant Stones from the Hollowdeep sustain the Veil's field. Without continuous supply, the field weakens, the Fallows equilibrium pressure increases, and the Veil fails—consuming Solasfall at the same accelerated rate as the rest of the world. The Hollowdeep rift is therefore not economic convenience; it is existential necessity.

## Controlled vs Natural
Natural rifts self-sustain. Controlled rifts close when mana input stops; indefinite maintenance requires coordinated capacity beyond current institutional limits. Small-scale spatial rifts—brief, localised tears—can be created without a weak point, but are limited in range and duration by the mana needed to overcome local spatial resistance.
