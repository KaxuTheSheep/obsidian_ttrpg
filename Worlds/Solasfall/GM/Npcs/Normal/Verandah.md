**Official Title:** None — self-styled "The Nexus Peddler" 
**Role:** Independent Hollowdeep merchant. Buys relics, stones, and salvage off explorers cheap, deep in the Hollowdeep where they have no other buyer and no leverage, then resells the same stock back topside at a markup once it's safely out. 
**Status:** Alive
**Race:** Kenku
**Languages:** Common, Cawscript, a mangled but enthusiastic smattering of Solari

## Name
Kenku take names from whatever they've mimicked and liked the sound of. Verandah heard "verandah" from a noble muttering about their estate's architecture during a market haggle, decided it sounded rich and important, and has used it ever since — he has no idea what it actually means and gets visibly annoyed if anyone tells him.

## Overview
Verandah operates on the simplest business model in the Hollowdeep: desperation is a discount. He sets up shop just inside the safer early nexuses — most often [[The Sunken Hollow]] — because that's as far as he's personally willing to risk his own feathers, and because it's exactly the point in an expedition where explorers are either flush with early finds and eager to lighten their load, or already rattled and looking to offload risk for coin before pressing deeper. He buys everything at a fraction of Guild or Market District value, citing "convenience," "risk," and "my generous nature," then hauls it back to Solasfall to sell at standard or inflated prices to people who never had to set foot in the Hollowdeep at all.

He is loud, theatrical, and utterly shameless about the markup — he'll tell you exactly how much profit he's making off you mid-transaction, framed as a compliment to your business sense for "understanding the trade." Most explorers hate him a little and use him anyway, because a bad price now beats carrying a heavier pack through whatever's in the next tunnel.

## Personality
- **Performatively grand.** Every sentence has the energy of a market crier. He gestures big, projects his voice, and treats every haggle like a stage performance he's certain he's winning.
- **Unbothered by being disliked.** He knows explorers resent him and finds it funny rather than wounding — "you'll be back," delivered with total confidence, because they always are.
- **Magpie instincts dressed up as taste.** He genuinely likes shiny, ornate, or strange things and will occasionally lowball an item not for profit but because he simply wants it for himself, then get defensive if pressed on why he won't resell it.
- **Fake nobility, sincerely performed.** He affects noble mannerisms — a limp-wristed flourish, a mangled attempt at formal Solari, exaggerated diction — despite Kenku having no standing in noble circles whatsoever. He is not being ironic. He thinks it works.
- **Fascinated, not fearful, of Hollowdeep horror.** Where most explorers want mimic nobles dead or avoided, Verandah wants to _meet one_. This is his one genuinely strange character trait and worth playing up — it makes him bizarrely brave or bizarrely stupid depending on the scene.

## The Mimic Noble Fascination
Verandah has become obsessed with the idea of a creature that perfectly impersonates nobility — to him this isn't a monster, it's the single greatest performance in the world, and he is desperate to see one do its act. He collects secondhand rumours of mimic sightings from other explorers, always asks travelling parties if they've seen one, and has started a running "if you find one, don't kill it, come get me first" request that nobody has taken seriously yet.

He does not fully grasp — or willfully ignores — that a mimic noble is a predator that eats people who fall for the act. Playing this as endearing naivety rather than idiocy works best: he's not unaware it's dangerous, he's just decided the performance is worth the risk, the same logic that gets him to buy junk he can't resell.

## Mercenaries
Verandah never sets up shop without two hired guards, paid a flat rate per expedition rather than a cut of profit (he's explicit about this arrangement to anyone who asks — he doesn't want them thinking they're owed a percentage). He treats them as staff, not friends, though he's not unkind to them. Their names and exact stat blocks are left open for whichever session needs them — reskin as needed:

- One built for standing between Verandah and trouble (a Marauder Vanguard– or Marauder Hauler–tier melee statline works fine).
- One built for spotting trouble before it arrives (a Wayfinder-adjacent or ranged/Perception-leaning statline).

Neither is particularly loyal beyond the coin — if a fight looks unwinnable, don't play them as suicidal on Verandah's behalf.

## Objectives (for roleplay hooks)
1. **The Deal.** His baseline goal every scene — buy cheap, sell high, keep the conversation entertaining enough that people come back even knowing they're being fleeced.
2. **The Mimic.** His actual passion project. Any rumour, sighting, or lead on a mimic noble will visibly derail him from business — he'll drop his asking price, offer information for free, or beg to tag along, entirely out of character for his usual stinginess.
3. **The Collection.** He's quietly keeping a small personal hoard of items he's decided are "too good to sell" — mostly noble-adjacent trinkets, fine jewelry, anything with a flourish. A party that notices this and comments on it can get him rambling, or defensive if they push to buy a piece he's decided is his.
4. **Reputation Building.** He wants to be known — "the" name explorers think of before they hit deeper nexuses. He'll fish for compliments, ask parties to "mention him" to others, and occasionally offer a slightly better price to a well-known or memorable party purely for the story value.

## Quick Roleplay Hooks

- Opens conversation with a wildly overstated greeting, usually addressing the party by an invented noble-sounding title.
- Haggling with him is genuinely fun rather than adversarial — he enjoys the back-and-forth and will occasionally give ground just because a good counter-offer amused him if the reasoning is interesting.
- If a party brings up mimics at all, even in passing, he will not let the topic go for the rest of the scene.
- Will absolutely try to buy something a player clearly doesn't want to sell, "just to ask."
