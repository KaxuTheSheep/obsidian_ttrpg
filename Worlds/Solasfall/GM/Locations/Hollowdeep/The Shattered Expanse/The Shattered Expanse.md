### Overview
The Shattered Expanse is the next major progression tier of the Hollowdeep — a vast petrified forest enclosed in a 500m × 500m cube roughly the height of a mature oak (~25–30m). Every surface — floor, walls, ceiling — is a **membrane**: a taut, water-like skin reflecting an inverted, mirrored version of the space on its far side, offset in time. Where the Sunken Hollow is a straightforward cave system with restrained strangeness, the Shattered Expanse is the opposite — a nexus built almost entirely around a single unsettling rule (you are never alone; a version of you is always on the other side of the membrane) expressed six different ways depending on which face you're standing near.

This is intended to feel disorienting rather than merely dangerous. Combat here is real, but the greater threat is psychological — watching your own reflection move wrong, hearing your own voice arrive early, or realising the version of events you just lived through has quietly rewritten itself.

---

## The Central Cube
The entry area and the calmest space in the Expanse, insofar as anywhere here is calm. A petrified forest of stone oak-trunks stands beneath a ceiling membrane that mirrors the floor directly above, upside-down, running **3 seconds behind** real time. Anyone below can watch their own reflection moving on the "ceiling" a few seconds delayed.

Four wall membranes, one per cardinal direction, lead to an altered variant of this same cube — see **The Four Faces** below. Travelling back the way you came from any face returns you to the Central Cube; travelling onward in any other direction from within a face carries you out of the Expanse entirely (see **Portals**).

## Membrane Mechanics

### Tension & Passage
Membranes are physically passable but resist crossing with tension — enough to stop casual contact, not enough to stop genuine intent.
- **Deliberate passage:** committing real force (a Full Action, or an equivalent narrative commitment — a shove, a dive, a weapon thrust) succeeds automatically. Tension here is a formality, not a barrier.
- **Accidental passage:** being forced through unintentionally — shoved, knocked back, stumbling, falling — requires the membrane to "catch" the character: **Grace or Might Test (DN 14)**. Success means the membrane holds and the character does not go through; failure means they're pulled to the other side.

### Reflection Linkage
Every character has a mirrored counterpart standing on the linked side of whichever membrane they're nearest. Positional force — hits, pushes, falls, knockback — transfers to the counterpart in **mirrored direction**: struck on the right, the counterpart is struck on the left, and so on.

If the counterpart lacks the physical means to be affected identically (missing a weapon the original still holds, standing where the terrain differs), **the sensation and damage still occur as though they did.** This is a linkage of force and consequence, not a literal transfer of equipment — a disarmed reflection does not gain a phantom weapon's damage output, it simply still hurts exactly as if it had been struck by one.

### Retrocausal Reach
Because each linked reflection runs on a fixed time offset, it is possible to take an action now aimed at altering what your reflection did within that offset window — reaching into your own recent past or near future and changing it.

When this happens, the GM narrates the intervening seconds as having **always occurred the new way** — minor state, positioning, and memory adjust retroactively to match. This is not adjudicated as a branching timeline; there is only ever one version of events, and it is whichever version currently exists after the change. Every character who witnessed the *original* version takes Sanity damage per the Reflection Sanity rule below — the vertigo of watching one's own memory quietly rewrite itself is exactly the kind of thing that breaks minds in this place.

### Reflection Sanity
Looking directly at one's own membrane-reflections compounds with exposure. At GM discretion, call for a **Resolve Test (DN 14)**. On failure, the character takes Sanity damage equal to **2 × the number of Reflections currently visible to them** — two mirrored copies of self in view deals 4 Sanity, and so on. This should be called sparingly but escalate naturally: a single reflection early in the session barely registers; a party caught at a corner where three membranes overlap should feel it.

## The Four Faces
Each cardinal wall of the Central Cube leads to a variant cube defined by one committed, uncompromising physical rule. Travelling inward (back the direction you entered) returns to the Central Cube. Travelling in **any other direction** warps around that face's assigned rift connection, carrying the party out of the Shattered Expanse entirely and into the linked nexus — see **Portals**.

### West — The Elastic Face
Every physical property is elastic: ground, stone trunks, and membranes alike behave like taut rubber. Falls bounce rather than deal falling damage up to a GM-set threshold; thrown weapons and projectiles rebound unpredictably; melee committed too hard can send both combatants recoiling. Reflection Linkage force transfitted through an elastic membrane may amplify rather than transfer 1:1 — GM discretion on degree, but err toward chaotic and mildly comedic rather than lethal, to keep this face distinct in tone from South.

### South — The Inverted Face
Gravity and rotation are flipped entirely. What would be the ceiling is the walkable floor here, and characters physically stand on the membrane itself rather than beneath it, looking "down" into the Central Cube's own sky. Time on this face runs **+3 seconds ahead** of the Central Cube — meaning from the Central Cube's perspective, looking up at the south membrane shows events that haven't happened yet from the Cube's own frame, while from the South face, looking down shows the Cube's own past. This keeps the single derived rule intact: the far side of any membrane always runs 3 seconds behind the near side's own perspective.

### North — The Farsight Face
The one deliberate exception to the standard offset. North's membrane shows **+10 seconds into the future** rather than the usual ±3 — but only as pure information. Reflection Linkage and Retrocausal Reach both remain bound to the normal 3-second window regardless of what's visible; the extra 7 seconds can be seen and acted on tactically (a genuine early warning), but cannot be reached into or altered. North is the nexus's oracle, on a leash — players can use it to see trouble coming, but not to reach forward and stop it directly. Physical properties otherwise match the Central Cube.

### East — The Scalebent Face
Crossing into this face doubles or halves scale — GM's call per crossing, or roll — affecting the character, their gear, and their immediate surroundings' proportions relative to the rest of the face. This meaningfully disrupts Reach, Close-Quarters engagement, and Carrying Capacity for the duration (treat as the relevant Size Category shift per [[Size Categories]] — Half-Step for a single doubling/halving). Reflection Linkage across a Scalebent membrane transfers force at the character's *true* scale, not their altered one, which can produce a badly mismatched — and unsettling — hit.

## Portals
Per the existing Hollowdeep connectivity (see Hollowdeep.canvas):
- **Central Cube ↔** one assigned nexus rift per cardinal face — recommended: West → The Iron Ravine, South → The Obsidian Glade, North → The Sundered Marsh, East → The Glass Expanse (reassign freely to fit table needs).
- Within any face-variant, the return-to-centre wall leads back to the Central Cube. The other three walls all converge on that face's assigned nexus rift — no matter which "wrong" direction a party pushes through out there, the distorted space delivers them to the same destination.

## Tone
The Shattered Expanse should read as quietly wrong rather than overtly monstrous — a place where the geometry itself is the antagonist more often than any creature in it. Combat exists and matters, but the nexus's real teeth are psychological: doubt about which reflection is real, dread at a membrane's whisper, and the low-grade horror of realising a moment the party lived through didn't happen quite the way they remember it now. Reserve the heaviest Reflection Sanity hits and Retrocausal Reach set-pieces for moments the GM wants to land hard — overusing either dulls the effect fast.
