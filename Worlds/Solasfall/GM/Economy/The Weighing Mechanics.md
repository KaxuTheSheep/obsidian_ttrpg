This document extends [[The Weighing]] with the mechanical tax deduction, payment schedule, and consequences for non-payment. See [[The Weighing]] for the narrative and social framing; this page covers only the numbers.

## Payment Schedule
The Weighing occurs **two weeks after each Rift opening** — every 4-week [[Rift]] cycle, in line with [[Hollowdeep]] expedition timing. Every character, player or otherwise, owes a tax on this schedule regardless of whether they personally went into the Hollowdeep that cycle.

## Calculating the Tax
**Tax Owed = Base Fee + (Percentage × Current Gilded Mints held)**

Holdings are counted at the moment the Weighing is called — Gilded Mints on hand or in any accessible account, not the value of carried equipment, Mana Stones not yet converted, or property. This is deliberate: it means a party that returns from an expedition flush with coin and doesn't spend or convert it before the Weighing pays substantially more, while a party that banks their wealth in gear, stones, or property ahead of time reduces their exposure. Expect players to start timing their spending around the Rift cycle once they catch on — that's the intended tension, not an exploit to close.

### Base Fee and Percentage by District
| District of Residence | Base Fee (Gilded Mints) | Percentage of Holdings |
|---|---|---|
| Slum District | 15 | 6% |
| Racial Districts | 20 | 7% |
| Human Commoner District | 25 | 8% |
| Entertainment District | 35 | 10% |
| Market District | 40 | 10% |
| Noble District | 60 | 14% |

The percentage scales by district as well as the base fee, in keeping with the Ministry's "leverage" framing — a Noble isn't just assumed to hold more, they're taxed proportionally harder on what they hold, since public non-payment costs them more in standing than it does a Slum resident who has little standing left to lose. **If this feels like it's doing too much at once, the simplest fix is flattening the percentage column to a single flat rate (e.g. 8% for everyone) and keeping only the Base Fee scaled by district** — worth deciding once you've run a session or two of this and seen how it actually plays.

> **Example:** A Slum District character holding 200 Mints at the Weighing owes 15 + (6% × 200) = 15 + 12 = **27 Gilded Mints**. A Noble holding the same 200 Mints owes 60 + (14% × 200) = 60 + 28 = **88 Gilded Mints**.
> **Example:** That same Slum character returns from a good haul holding 1,000 Mints instead — they now owe 15 + 60 = **75 Gilded Mints**, nearly triple, purely because they didn't spend or convert their earnings first.

## Payment
Tax may be paid in Gilded Mints directly, or in [[Mana Stones]] converted at the reference rates below — the Ministry accepts stones at verified assay points, but a party paying in raw stones during the Weighing itself (rather than pre-converting) takes a **10% penalty to the stone's converted value**, representing the Ministry's on-the-spot appraisal squeeze rather than a fair assay.

## Consequences of Non-Payment
Failure to pay in full by the Weighing's Execution Day (one week before the next Rift opens) triggers, in order:

1. **Partial payment.** If a character pays at least half their owed tax, the shortfall is logged as **Debt** (see below) rather than triggering arrest.
2. **Debt.** Unpaid amounts carry forward and accrue **+10% per missed cycle**. A character with active Debt suffers **Disadvantage on all Negotiation Tests within Solasfall** — word travels through the Ministry's registers, and merchants, landlords, and contacts treat a debtor as a bad risk.
3. **Conscription or arrest.** If a character misses **two consecutive cycles** without partial payment, the Order of the Gilded Chain moves to collect. The GM adjudicates the specific outcome narratively (per [[The Weighing]]'s existing Execution Day framing), but mechanically this should mean one of: forced enrolment into a Guild-adjacent expedition to work off the debt, seizure of equipment up to the Debt's value, or — for a character with no ability to pay at all and no leverage to offer — the standard Execution Day consequence described in the base document.

Debt is tracked per character, not per party. A party member covering a companion's shortfall is a roleplay choice, not an automatic pooling of resources.

## Reference Pricing — Mana Stones
Verified assay point conversion rates, current as of standard Ministry pegging. These fluctuate narratively at GM discretion (the Ministry "continually recalculates" per [[Mana Stones]]) — treat the table as a baseline, not a fixed law.

| Stone Type | Weak Excitation | Moderate Excitation | Strong Excitation | Extreme Excitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vivant Stone | 15 Mints | 40 Mints | 90 Mints | 200 Mints |
| Mordant Stone | 20 Mints | 55 Mints | 120 Mints | 260 Mints |
| Inert Stone | 3 Mints | — | — | — |

Mordant Stones price consistently higher than Vivant at equivalent intensity, reflecting their direct demand for [[Veil]] maintenance — the Ministry pays a premium because it needs the supply guaranteed, not because Mordant stones are inherently rarer. Inert Stones have no excitation band; they sell only as base crystalline material, mostly to Runeweavers or Aetherbron for structural components rather than as fuel.

**GM guidance for assigning intensity:** Weak/Moderate stones are the realistic yield of a Level 0–2 party's early finds; Strong stones should feel like a genuine haul; Extreme stones are rare enough to be a plot-relevant discovery in their own right, not a routine drop.
